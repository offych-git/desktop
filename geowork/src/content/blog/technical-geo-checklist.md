Before investing heavily in new content, check whether the information already on your website is easy to reach, understand, and trust.

Technical Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is not about adding a secret file that forces AI platforms to recommend your business. It is about making important information clear and accessible.

## 1. Confirm the basics

Check that critical pages return an appropriate success response and are not unintentionally blocked.

- Make sure your key services are accessible through ordinary links.
- Keep an XML sitemap updated with canonical, indexable URLs.
- Review robots.txt and page-level robots directives.
- Fix broken internal links and unnecessary redirect chains.
- Ensure core information is available without requiring complex client-side interactions.

A clean structure helps both people and automated systems navigate your site.

## 2. Describe your company consistently

Your homepage and About page should communicate what your company actually does, where it operates, who it serves, and how to contact it.

If you have locations, present their correct details. If you have named experts, provide verifiable biographies rather than generic claims.

Consistency across your own site and legitimate external profiles can make the company's identity easier to interpret.

## 3. Use structured data appropriately

Schema.org structured data can clarify the relationships between entities such as an Organization, WebSite, Service, Article, or Person.

Only publish properties that reflect visible, factual information. Do not add invented reviews, ratings, author credentials, or claims.

Structured data is a descriptive aid—not a guarantee of placement in AI answers.

## 4. Make each service page useful

For important services, explain:

- Who the service is for.
- What is included and what is not.
- How the process works.
- What a prospective buyer should consider.
- How a customer can ask for more information.

Answer questions directly, then add context. Avoid padding paragraphs with repetitive keywords.

## 5. Establish editorial credibility

For articles, use accurate dates, relevant sources, and transparent attribution. Correct outdated information. Where expertise matters, show the real qualifications of the people responsible for the material.

Credibility is not something that a schema property can create on its own.

## 6. Treat optional machine-readable files sensibly

Files such as llms.txt may help document a site's intended content structure for certain tools, but support varies. They are not a universal ranking factor. A readable website and useful pages remain more important.

If your site has extensive catalogs, locations, or services, carefully designed JSON endpoints may be helpful for integrations and discovery.

## 7. Verify after publishing

Revisit crawl access, canonical tags, structured data validity, page content and analytics after making changes. Technical work is valuable when it remains correct.
