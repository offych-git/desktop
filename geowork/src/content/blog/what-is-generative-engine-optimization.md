Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the process of improving how accurately and frequently a brand is represented when AI systems answer questions. Instead of focusing only on a traditional search-results page, GEO considers the full answer a buyer receives.

Imagine a customer asking an AI assistant: *“Which service providers should I consider for my company?”* The assistant may summarize a category, compare companies, and link to sources. If your business information is difficult to access or understand, it may be left out.

GEO works to reduce that gap. It cannot guarantee a recommendation, but it can improve the quality and clarity of the information available about your business.

## GEO and SEO: what changes?

Traditional search engine optimization (SEO) focuses on discoverability and performance in search results. It includes technical crawling, helpful pages, internal links, and external credibility.

GEO builds on that foundation and adds a different question: **What does an AI-generated answer say about us?**

To answer it, a business needs to understand:

- Which questions prospective customers ask.
- Whether the brand appears in AI answers to those questions.
- Which competitors are mentioned instead.
- Which sources the answer uses or cites.
- Whether the information shown is accurate and current.

Search engine optimization is still important. GEO is not a replacement for accessible pages, useful content, or legitimate expertise.

## Three foundations of GEO

### 1. Be found

Key service pages should be accessible to crawlers. A clean sitemap, sensible internal navigation, working canonical URLs, and an absence of unnecessary indexing restrictions can help search and AI systems access the information they need.

### 2. Be understood

Every important page should explain its topic clearly. Describe services using customer language, name the geographic markets you serve, and make important details explicit. Structured data can clarify entities and relationships, but it does not guarantee that an AI model will use or cite a page.

### 3. Be trusted

Strong claims should be supported by evidence. Show actual experience, make authorship transparent, keep business details consistent, and earn relevant third-party mentions. Do not manufacture reviews, testimonials, or citations.

## A simple GEO starting checklist

1. Write down 10 customer questions that matter to revenue.
2. Check answers across a few AI search experiences.
3. Record which brands appear and which sources are cited.
4. Review whether your website directly answers those questions.
5. Improve weak pages with specific facts, helpful explanations, and credible sources.
6. Repeat the same checks over time.

This is a measurement exercise, not a one-time test. AI answers can vary from one session to another and across platforms.

## What success should look like

Useful measures include visibility within a consistent prompt set, citation sources, accurate descriptions of your services, and qualified traffic when that traffic is observable in analytics. These measurements offer a clearer picture than a promise to “rank #1 in ChatGPT.”
